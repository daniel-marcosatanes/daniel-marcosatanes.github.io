        <h3 style="font-weight: 700; color: var(--chem-teal); font-size: 2rem;">Don't Recycle It. Upcycle It.</h3>
        <p style="font-size: 1.2rem; line-height: 1.6;">
            Poly(vinyl chloride) (PVC) is notoriously difficult to recycle. Mechanical grinding degrades it. Chemical burning destroys it.
            <strong>We asked: Can we use organometallic catalysis to turn waste pipes into something <em>more</em> valuable?</strong>
        </p>

        <h4 style="color: var(--chem-teal);">Precision Editing of Polymers</h4>
        <p>In the <strong>Hartwig Lab (UC Berkeley)</strong>, we developed a Copper-catalyzed method to surgically replace inert C–Cl bonds with boronic esters.</p>
        <p>Unlike traditional methods that break the polymer chain, our catalyst modifies the surface while keeping the material's integrity intact. We turned inert plastic into a reactive canvas.</p>

        <h3 style="font-weight: 700;">Stronger than Superglue</h3>
        <p>The result wasn't just a new plastic. It was a <strong>powerful adhesive</strong>.</p>
        <p>The new boron groups act like molecular anchors, bonding covalently to glass and metal. To prove it, we took shavings from a used PVC pipe, modified them, and used them to glue two aluminum bars.</p>
